Written by Chad Lawson

## Genres and Tropes
If one were to ask, "What would happen if you took a hyper-secretive, backstabbing, insane TTRPG like [Paranoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoia_(role-playing_game)) and put it in a [telepod](https://ultimatepopculture.fandom.com/wiki/Telepods) with a super-open game toolbox like [Cortex Prime](https://www.cortexrpg.com)?"

_Cortex Paranoia_ would answer, "Why do you think you have clearance to know that, citizen?! If that was something you should know, clearly **The Computer** would have told you, right? _**RIGHT?!**_" And then it would zap you. ...and maybe your next clone as well, just to be sure.

### Missing Information or Misinformation?
At the start of the game, your character sheet is going to be fairly blank. Every roll you make not only determines if you get what you want, but has a pretty good chance of filling in one of those blank spaces in the process. ...as well as getting you labeled as a traitor and killed.

### Decentralized Authority
In _Cortex Paranoia_, there is no _single_ GM. Rather each of the players take turns acting as **The Computer** for the characters in the scene.

### Inspirations
You mean besides the original _Paranoia_ TTRPG? All five books of _The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy_ trilogy are a good starting point. Also most [Terry Gilliam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gilliam_filmography) movies such as _"[Time Bandits](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0081633)"_ or _"[Monty Python and the Holy Grail](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0071853)"_.

The two mods (Character Creation and The Treason Die) are inspired by [The Leverage RPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage:_The_Roleplaying_Game) by Cam Banks and Rob Donoghue and the [Strage Towns](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/567043/strange-towns) Spotlight by Kira Magrann respectively.

## Rules and Variants
_Cortex Paranoia_ uses **distinctions**, **attributes**, and **relationships** as prime traits. Relationships use **trait statements**. Each scene consists of a **crisis pool** that is resolved with **contests** and follow **dramatic order**. 

Distinctions have **SFX**. Each character has access to **resources** which replenish when they die and their new clone arrives.

Physical damage as well as existential problems are all tracked as **complications**. If a complication gets stepped up beyond d12, that clone dies and the next one in the six-pack shows up in the next scene.

### Contests Over Tests
Each roll should be a contest rather than a test. Everything a character tries to do will be met with persistent resistance whether it be nature, fate, another character, or The Computer. The initiating character may choose to **give in** but the opposition never will.

### Distinctions
Characters have three distinctions: _Service Group_, _Mutant Power_, and _Secret Society_. Each of which has the standard "Hinder" SFX. Each has another SFX that unlocks when it is used for the first time.

### Treason Die
Every character has a **treason die** that starts at d4. When you use your distinctions' SFX, you add your treason die to the pool and then step it up. If it would be stepped up beyond d12, a brief scene should take place where your treason is discovered, confronted, and your chracter is killed. When your clone arrives, reset the treason die to d4.

## Character Creation
At the start of the game, you only know two things: your name (see below) and that you implicitly trust the other troubleshooters in your party. Everything else is above your security clearance and on a need-to-know basis.

When (not if) your clone is killed, your next clone in the pack arrives with a fresh character sheet. Increase their clone identity (see below) and set all their relationships back to d12 (also below).

### Character Names
Character names have the following format: XXX-C-RES-n, where:
- XXX is your short name
- C is your clearance code (starts at 'R' for RED)
- RES is a three letter code for your residential unit
- n is your clone identity between 1-6 and start with 1

**Examples:**
```
Toyz-R-USS-1
Pep-R-MNT-1
Libb-R-TEE-1
Teach-R-PET-1
Ewe-R-SUS-1
```

### Relationships
Each character starts with a d12 relationship to each of the other PCs with the trait statement of **"TCATAITTCSITT"** (The Computer Assigned Them And I Trust The Computer So I Trust Them).

### And the rest?
Hey! Are you questioning The Computer, citizen?! Are ya?! Yeah, I didn't think so. Leave it blank for now.

## Running the Game

### Trust the Computer
_Cortex Paranoia_ uses **dramatic order**. At the start of a game, everyone roll a d20 and the player with the lowest roll is _The Computer_ first.

When action is passed from one player to the next, the player who just acted becomes _The Computer_ for them, and so on.

### Everything's a crisis
Working for the computer is dangerous. At the start of the game, players should determine how many tasks they want to take on and roll on the _task table_ that many times. If the same number comes up multiple times, you can re-roll or let it stand.

### Getting Suspicious
During play, if you are helping or hindering another PC in a scene and you question that relationship trait statement, you can triple that die for this roll and then step it down. (CPGH page 65) Create a new statement to reflect why they are sus.

If you challenge the statement attached to a d4 relationship, after the roll you are now 100% proof-positive _convinced_ that the other character is a traitor. A brief scene should take place where that PC is confronted and killed for for their treachery.

When their clone arrives, reset the relationship to d12 with the **"TCATAITTCSITT"** statement.
