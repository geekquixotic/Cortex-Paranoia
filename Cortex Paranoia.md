## Genres and Tropes
If one were to ask, "What would happen if you took a hyper-secretive, backstabbing, insane TTRPG like [Paranoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoia_(role-playing_game)) and put it in a [telepod](https://ultimatepopculture.fandom.com/wiki/Telepods) with a super-open game toolbox like [Cortex Prime](https://www.cortexrpg.com)?"

_Cortex Paranoia_ would answer, "Why do you think you have clearance to know that, citizen?! If that was something you should know, clearly **The Computer** would have told you, right? _**RIGHT?!**_" And then it would zap you. ...and maybe your next clone as well, just to be sure.

### Missing Information or Misinformation
At the start of the game, your character sheet is going to be fairly blank. Every roll you make not only determines if you get what you want, but has a pretty good chance of filling in one of those blank spaces in the process. ...as well as getting you labeled as a traitor and killed.

### Decentralized Authority
In _Cortex Paranoia_, there is no _single_ GM. Rather each of the players take turns acting as **The Computer** for the characters in the scene.

### Inspirations
You mean besides the original _Paranoia_ TTRPG? All five books of _The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy_ trilogy are a good starting point. Also most [Terry Gilliam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gilliam_filmography) movies such as _"[Time Bandits](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0081633)"_ or _"[Monty Python and the Holy Grail](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0071853)"_.

The two mods (Character Creation and The Treason Die) are inspired by [The Leverage RPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage:_The_Roleplaying_Game) by Cam Banks and Rob Donoghue and the [Strage Towns](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/567043/strange-towns) Spotlight by Kira Magrann.

## Rules and Variants
_Cortex Paranoia_ uses **distinctions**, **attributes**, and **relationships** as prime traits. Relationships use **trait statements**. Each scene consists of a **crisis pool** that is resolved with **contests** and follow **dramatic order**. 

Physical damage as well as existential problems are all tracked as **complications**. If a complication gets stepped up beyond d12, that clone dies and the next one in the six-pack shows up in the next scene.

### Contests Over Tests
Each roll should be a contest rather than a test. Everything a character tries to do will be met with persistent resistance whether it be nature, fate, another character, or The Computer. The initiating character may choose to **give in** but the opposition never will.

### Treason Die
Every character has a **treason die** that starts at d6.
